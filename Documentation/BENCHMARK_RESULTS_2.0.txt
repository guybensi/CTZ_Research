GUY BENCHMARK RESTRICTED TO THE ALL_SKY_AIFLUX 37-FEATURE POOL
================================================================================
Date: 2026-08-02

Command:
python3 python/guys_benchmark_models.py --feature-selection importance \
  --feature-source allsky37 --importance-models Ridge \
  --data-root data/paired/Pacific_2014-2015 \
  --output-dir benchmark_outputs/guys_benchmark_allsky37_full

--- What changed vs. the original guy_model_1.0.py recipe ---
guys_benchmark_models.py was extended with a --feature-source allsky37 mode
(load_allsky37_subset()) that restricts the candidate feature pool, *before*
any Random Forest importance ranking, to exactly the 37 features
All_Sky_AIflux.py hand-picks: SZA, VZA, RAA (viewing/solar geometry) plus mean
and skew spectral moments at its 17 fixed wavelengths (0.47, 0.66, 0.86, 1.24,
1.375, 2.13, 3.96, 4.05, 4.5, 6.7, 7.3, 8.5, 9.7, 11.0, 12.0, 13.33, 14.23 um),
each matched to the nearest real SW/LW channel per file - i.e. no Isw/Ilw
radiance scalars, no CF/meanCTH/stdCTH/AOD/Clrf/glintf/landf, no Lat/Lon, and
no std/kurtosis moments. SVM was dropped from the classical sweep
(--importance-models Ridge) since SVR does not scale to a multi-million-row
training set; this doesn't change the recommendation since
select_best_practical_model() already always picks Ridge for the practical
config regardless of SVM's score.

--- Data used ---
Folder: data/paired/Pacific_2014-2015/{moments,scalars}/ (same 2163 file pairs
  as section 1)
Rows kept after dropping any row with NaN/Inf across only these 37 features +
  target: X.shape=(5,260,208, 37), y.shape=(5,260,208,)
  -> ~3.1x more rows survive than guy_model_1.0.py's 129-feature version
     (1,703,803 rows) because far fewer columns can NaN-out a given FOV
Test split: 20% -> ~4,208,166 train rows / ~1,052,042 test rows

--- Answer ---
Yes, noticeably worse in absolute terms, even though R^2 looks almost
unchanged: RMSE/MAE roughly DOUBLE for Ridge (11.03 -> 22.58 W/m^2) and
roughly TRIPLE for the ANN (5.76 -> 15.73 W/m^2) when the pool is restricted
to moments + image/solar geometry only (no Isw/Ilw radiance, no
AOD/Clrf/CF/CTH cloud-aerosol scalars, no Lat/Lon). R^2 staying ~0.98-0.99 in
both cases is not a fair apples-to-apples signal here: the two runs are
scored against different row sets with different label variance (5.26M rows
here vs. 1.70M for the 129-feature version, since restricting to fewer
columns lets ~3x more FOVs survive the all-finite filter), so R^2 alone
understates the gap - RMSE/MAE in W/m^2 are the meaningful comparison.
The dominant loss driver is losing Isw (SW radiance/irradiance, the #2 most
important feature in the full 129-feature recipe) plus AOD/Clrf/Lat; within
the restricted 37-feature pool, a single channel (mean SW reflectance at
0.66um) ends up carrying ~93% of the RF importance on its own, well beyond
its role in the full recipe, since it has to substitute for information the
excluded scalars would otherwise provide.
